\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider our Data Descriptor entitled ``Chile-ED-Resp: A Reproducible Synthetic Weekly Benchmark Dataset for Respiratory Emergency-Demand Forecasting in Chile'' for publication in \textit{Data}.

The submission presents a deterministic, fully synthetic, and openly reusable weekly benchmark designed for transparent comparison of respiratory emergency-demand forecasting methods. The release contains 1728 site-week observations spanning nine synthetic hospital profiles and 192 epidemiological weeks per site, together with cause-specific respiratory counts, age-group totals, meteorological and calendar covariates, quality flags, a predefined chronological split, machine-readable metadata, a complete data dictionary, SHA-256 checksums, validation reports, baseline forecasts, and executable generation scripts.

The work is aligned with the journal's emphasis on transparent, reusable, and well-documented scientific data. Unlike the previously model-centered manuscript from which the data-design problem emerged, the present submission is organized as a Data Descriptor and places dataset provenance, reproducibility, technical validation, file organization, licensing, and appropriate reuse at the center of the contribution. The manuscript also cites recent Data Descriptors that informed the organization of the release package.

All observations are explicitly synthetic; no patient-level, identifiable, or real hospital-activity data are included. The dataset and documentation are released under CC BY 4.0, and the code is released under the MIT License. Before formal submission, the repository will be made public on GitHub and archived in Zenodo, and the final persistent DOI will replace the placeholders currently included in the manuscript package.

The manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare no conflicts of interest.

Thank you for your consideration.
