\abstract{Respiratory emergencies create recurrent seasonal pressure on hospital services, yet reproducible forecasting research is often hindered by the effort required to retrieve, interpret, harmonize, and document public administrative data. Chile-ED-Resp is a curated secondary dataset and reproducible processing pipeline derived from the official Chilean Ministry of Health/DEIS dataset ``Atenciones de urgencias de causas respiratorias por semana epidemiol\'ogica'', produced by the Sistema de Atenci\'on Diaria de Urgencias (SADU). Version 1.1.0 uses the closed 2022--2025 period and was built from the official Parquet snapshot retrieved on 30 August 2026. After restriction to hospitals and hospital emergency units (UEH), the curated long artifact contains 449,844 rows and 32 variables, representing 180 hospitals and 12 official respiratory cause labels. A complementary forecasting artifact contains 37,620 hospital-week rows and 38 variables on a fixed 209-position statistical-week calendar (52 weeks in 2022--2024 and 53 in 2025); 178 of 180 hospitals (98.9\%) satisfy the predefined $\geq95\%$ target-coverage criterion. Acute upper respiratory infection is retained under its official Spanish label and acronym, IRA Alta. Missing source records remain missing and are distinguished from explicitly reported zeros. Precomputed target-derived predictors use outcomes available through the preceding week and are intended for rolling-origin one-week-ahead evaluation, not an unqualified fixed-origin forecast of the entire test year. Reproducibility is supported through source metadata, SHA-256 checksums, machine-readable descriptive audits, 15 validation checks, unit tests, and an example that recomputes predictors at each forecast origin. The resource provides a transparent foundation for forecasting, benchmarking, health-informatics teaching, and secondary analyses while preserving source provenance and licensing conditions.}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

Hospital emergency departments must absorb demand that changes with epidemiological conditions, season, age structure, and local service organization. Respiratory illness is particularly relevant because recurrent winter epidemics can concentrate consultations and hospitalizations within a limited number of weeks. Forecasting approaches have consequently been used to anticipate emergency attendance, bed demand, and severe respiratory hospitalizations \citep{Lucini2020,Duarte2021,Albrecht2024,Ackermann2025}. Methodological work on emergency-department demand forecasting spans early systematic reviews of statistical models \citep{Wargon2009} through unified and multivariable forecasting pipelines for hourly and daily arrivals and occupancy \citep{Vollmer2021,RostamiTabar2024,Tuominen2024IJF,PortoFogliatto2024,Alenezi2026}. Recent systematic reviews show both the growing use of machine-learning methods in emergency-demand prediction and persistent challenges related to external validation, generalizability, and operational integration \citep{Blanco2026,Hosseini2026}. Pathogen-specific forecasting systems have likewise targeted influenza, respiratory syncytial virus, and combined winter respiratory pressure on hospitals \citep{Mellor2023,Mellor2025,Cutrera2024,ChenXiao2024}. In Chile, empirical analyses have documented temporal variation in emergency consultations and pronounced respiratory-season effects \citep{ToroCastillo2024,DiazCarvajal2025}. Complementary Chilean clinical guidance and recent pediatric commentary describe the evolving respiratory-syncytial-virus and severe acute respiratory-infection context relevant to seasonal preparedness \citep{ZepedaF2024,DiazRubio2026}.

Within this setting, the Chilean Ministry of Health makes weekly respiratory emergency data publicly available through the Departamento de Estad\'isticas e Informaci\'on de Salud (DEIS). The source dataset, produced from the Sistema de Atenci\'on Diaria de Urgencias (SADU), reports consultations and hospitalizations by respiratory cause, establishment, statistical week, and age group \citep{DEIS2026}. Although the source is highly valuable, downstream forecasting studies must repeatedly solve the same data-engineering tasks: retrieving a large Parquet file, harmonizing field names and types, isolating hospital emergency units, separating consultations from hospitalization rows, checking temporal coverage, pivoting causes to hospital-week features, constructing lagged predictors without leakage, and documenting provenance.

From a data-publication perspective, the principal contribution of \datasetname{} is therefore not a new predictive algorithm. Instead, the resource provides a transparent and reusable research layer over the official source. The design follows the FAIR principles of findability, accessibility, interoperability, and reusability \citep{Wilkinson2016}, including implementation practices such as machine-readable metadata, versioned provenance, and automated validation \citep{Inau2023,Bahim2020,Devaraju2021Patterns,Devaraju2021DSJ,Groth2020,Fenner2019}. Table~\ref{tab:related-descriptors} now compares the release with the closest Chilean and Latin American emergency or respiratory resources rather than with datasets from unrelated domains.

Accordingly, \datasetname{} releases two complementary representations of the same official 2022--2025 respiratory emergency information. The first is a curated long-format table that preserves all qualifying hospital emergency-unit records and official count values. The second is a hospital-week forecasting artifact that reorganizes selected consultation causes and adds documented deterministic features. An explicit coverage criterion identifies establishments suitable for benchmarking rather than hard-coding a small set of hospitals. The repository records dimensions, inventories, descriptive audits, source checksum, retrieval time, and validation results, allowing later versions to remain auditable if the upstream portal revises historical records.

\section{Data Description}
\label{sec:data-description}

\subsection{Official Source and Scope}

The upstream resource is the government dataset \textit{Atenciones de urgencias de causas respiratorias por semana epidemiol\'ogica}, published through datos.gob.cl by the Chilean Ministry of Health/DEIS \citep{DEIS2026}. The active Parquet resource has identifier \texttt{\sourceid}. At the time this descriptor was prepared, the portal identified the resource under a Creative Commons Non-Commercial license; the linked terms correspond to Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 2.0 Generic (CC BY-NC 2.0) \citep{DEIS2026,CreativeCommonsBYNC20}. The source field is named \texttt{SemanaEstadistica}; consequently, the manuscript uses \emph{statistical week} for the analytical coordinate and retains \emph{epidemiological week} only when reproducing the official Spanish dataset title. Because the current reporting year is continuously updated, the curated release intentionally uses the closed four-year interval 2022--2025. Restricting the versioned release to completed years avoids treating a partially reported current week as a genuine demand decline. This concern for completeness and timeliness mirrors broader open-government-data quality frameworks \citep{Vetro2016,Bhandari2021}.

The source schema contains 25 variables after normalization to snake-case names. These fields describe establishment, region, municipality, health service, establishment type, administrative dependency, level of care, emergency-care type, coordinates, complexity level, year, statistical week, cause code/order, cause label, total count, and five age-specific count fields. The official DEIS data dictionary documents the source schema and field definitions used to map the Parquet resource into the curated release \citep{DEISDictionary2024}.

\subsection{Curated Long Table}

The long table restricts the official source to the years 2022--2025, establishments classified as \texttt{Hospital}, and records whose emergency type contains the hospital-emergency designation \texttt{UEH}. Table~\ref{tab:filter-audit} reports the row count after every filtering step. The final 449,844 rows represent 180 hospitals and 12 official respiratory cause labels; no cause is removed from the long artifact. Consultation codes 3--11 and hospitalization codes 33--35 are identified from the official \texttt{orden\_causa}/\texttt{causa} combinations and stored in \texttt{measure\_type}. This order-aware rule prevents hospitalization totals from entering consultation predictors.

Beyond normalization of column names and coordinate types, official count fields are retained unchanged. Derived fields include a deterministic row identifier, \texttt{statistical\_period}, the fixed source-resource identifier, the sum of the five official age strata, the difference between that sum and \texttt{num\_total}, and a quality flag. All six official count variables are complete in the selected long table; their explicit-zero frequencies are reported in Table~\ref{tab:missing-zero}. Any future missing source value remains missing rather than being imputed. Such treatment supports transparent downstream handling of administrative health data \citep{Lin2026}.

The mapping is order-aware rather than a fuzzy text substitution: only consultation orders 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, and 11 receive canonical forecasting fields. Consequently, labels that contain similar respiratory or COVID-19 wording but belong to hospitalization orders 33--35 cannot enter the forecasting variables.

An absent hospital-week-cause record is interpreted as unavailable reporting, not as zero events. Explicit zeros are values for which the official source contains a record with \texttt{num\_total = 0}. Accordingly, the pivot retains absent records as \texttt{NA} and preserves recorded zeros as 0; these states are never treated as interchangeable. Table~\ref{tab:missing-zero} shows that every forecasting cause has 133 missing grid positions (0.35\%), while zero frequencies vary materially by cause.

\noindent\textbf{Mandatory interpretation rule.} In the released tables, \texttt{NA} means that no qualifying source record was available for that hospital--week--cause combination, whereas 0 means that a qualifying official row explicitly reported zero events. Users must not replace the former with the latter unless an external reporting model justifies that recoding.

Hospital selection for benchmarking is coverage-based. The denominator is the fixed 209-position release calendar, not the set of weeks in which IRA Alta happens to appear somewhere nationally. Of the 180 hospitals, 172 report IRA Alta in all 209 positions; six additional hospitals remain above the 95\% threshold with 204--208 observed target weeks. Consequently, 178 hospitals (98.9\%) receive \texttt{recommended\_for\_benchmark = True}. The other two hospitals have 178/209 (85.2\%) and 121/209 (57.9\%) observed positions. Across the complete $180\times209$ grid, 37,487 of 37,620 IRA Alta values are observed (99.65\% completeness). Within the recommended cohort, 37,188 of 37,202 values are observed (99.96\%), leaving 14 missing targets. Table~\ref{tab:coverage-distribution} summarizes the distribution.

\subsection{Schema Harmonization and Type Treatment}

Column names are normalized to lower-case snake case. Because public-data schemas can appear with either CamelCase or previously cleaned headings, the build script matches fields through an underscore-insensitive canonical key before checking for the complete expected 25-field schema. If an expected source field is absent, the build stops rather than guessing a replacement. Numeric count, year, week, and cause-order columns are explicitly parsed; coordinates are converted from decimal-comma text to numeric decimal-point representation. No other source values are standardized in a way that changes their substantive meaning.

The curation script also keeps an explicit cause inventory. Consultation versus hospitalization is assigned from the official cause-order group, and \texttt{canonical\_feature} is assigned only to consultation orders 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, and 11. The inventory records every official label, measure class, number of source rows, aggregate reported total, canonical field, inclusion status, and exclusion reason. Maintaining the original label alongside canonical features allows users to audit the mapping and rebuild alternative targets.

\subsection{Fixed Statistical-Week Calendar and Time Index}

The release calendar is constructed before inspecting hospital-specific target availability. The official DEIS coordinates present in the archived 2022--2025 source define weeks 1--52 for 2022, 2023, and 2024 and weeks 1--53 for 2025, yielding 209 expected positions. The build checks the observed national IRA Alta coordinate set against this complete grid, validates the week-53 transition, and crosses all 180 hospitals with the grid. A zero-based \texttt{time\_index} runs continuously from 0 to 208, and \texttt{weeks\_in\_statistical\_year} records whether the corresponding year contains 52 or 53 positions. Consequently, a week absent everywhere in a future source snapshot would remain an explicit all-missing grid position rather than causing later row-shift lags to cease representing consecutive calendar positions.

\subsection{Hospital Coverage and Selection}

Rather than selecting hospitals on the basis of peak demand, geographic convenience, or prior model performance, the release applies a transparent completeness criterion. Let $W^{*}$ denote the fixed set of 209 expected statistical-week positions and let $W_h\subseteq W^{*}$ denote positions with an observed IRA Alta value for hospital $h$. Coverage is
\begin{equation}
C_h = \frac{|W_h|}{|W^{*}|}=\frac{|W_h|}{209}.
\end{equation}
The fixed denominator prevents a nationally absent source record from inflating every hospital's coverage. The default benchmark recommendation is $C_h \geq 0.95$. On the 209-position calendar, this rule requires at least 199 observed target weeks and permits at most 10 missing weeks (4.8\%). It was specified as a completeness convention independently of forecasting outcomes. The observed coverage distribution provides an additional empirical check: the least-complete retained hospital has 204/209 weeks (97.6\%), whereas the most-complete excluded hospital has 178/209 weeks (85.2\%). Thus, the 0.95 cutoff lies inside an observed separation gap, and any threshold in $(178/209,\,204/209]$ yields the same 178-hospital benchmark cohort. This threshold is not a clinical quality criterion or a claim that 0.95 is universally optimal; it limits avoidable missingness while retaining 98.9\% of hospitals, and the full 180-hospital grid remains available for sensitivity analyses using alternative criteria.

\subsection{Feature Engineering Without Leakage}

\begingroup\sloppy
For a hospital $h$ and target series $y_{h,t}$ ordered by \texttt{time\_index}, short-term lag features are $y_{h,t-k}$ for $k \in \{1,2,4\}$. The year-over-year feature is not a fixed 52-row shift. For statistical year $a$ and statistical week $w$, it matches $y^{(\mathrm{prev\mbox{-}year})}_{h,a,w}=y_{h,a-1,w}$ and is therefore named \path{ira_alta_prev_year_same_week}. This definition handles the 2025 week-53 transition explicitly; where the preceding year lacks week 53 or the matched source value is unavailable, the feature is missing. Prior rolling means use
\begin{equation}
\bar{y}^{(m)}_{h,t}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}y_{h,t-j}, \quad m \in \{4,12\},
\end{equation}
whenever all required prior values exist. Because every hospital is crossed with the complete calendar before feature computation, $t-k$ denotes the exact preceding grid position rather than the $k$th preceding observed value. Thus, a missing $y_{h,t-k}$ produces a missing lag and is never bypassed. Rolling windows likewise operate on the preceding four or 12 grid positions with the required observation count equal to the window length; if any position in that window is missing, the rolling mean remains missing rather than shortening the window or skipping the gap. Boundary positions without sufficient history also remain missing. The current observation $y_{h,t}$ is excluded from each rolling feature.

Seasonal position is encoded as
\begin{equation}
s_t=\sin\left(\frac{2\pi (w_t-1)}{m_t}\right), \qquad c_t=\cos\left(\frac{2\pi (w_t-1)}{m_t}\right),
\end{equation}
where $w_t$ is the statistical-week number and $m_t\in\{52,53\}$ is the number of positions in that statistical year. In a 53-week statistical year, week 53 is retained as a distinct observation and is encoded at angle $2\pi(52/53)$, the last unique phase before week 1 of the next year returns to $(s_t,c_t)=(0,1)$. It is neither dropped nor duplicated as week 1. In a 52-week year, the denominator is 52; consequently, the same numbered week can have a slightly different normalized annual phase across 52- and 53-week years by design. Therefore, the implementation does not use a fixed denominator of 52: it reads \texttt{weeks\_in\_statistical\_year} for every row. The information set for rolling-origin one-week-ahead evaluation at origin $t$ is $\mathcal{I}_{h,t}=\{y_{h,s}:s<t\}$ together with non-target covariates available by $t$. The repository example reconstructs all target-derived predictors from $\mathcal{I}_{h,t}$ at every origin and appends $y_{h,t}$ to the history only after the forecast has been scored. For a fixed-origin horizon $H>1$, users must recursively update target-derived features or omit predictors unavailable at the origin; the precomputed 2025 columns do not by themselves establish leakage-free fixed-origin evaluation.
\endgroup

\subsection{Automated Build and Versioning}

The public repository contains Python scripts for source retrieval, curation, validation, descriptive audits, and rolling-origin evaluation. Unit tests cover schema normalization, cause/measure separation, the 209-position calendar, and continuous time indexing. The GitHub Actions workflow uses Python 3.12.13 and installs \texttt{requirements.lock} with \texttt{--require-hashes}; this file fixes the complete direct and transitive dependency graph to exact versions and verifies package hashes. The human-maintained \texttt{requirements.txt} retains only development-level minimum constraints and is not used for the reproducible workflow. The action then executes the tests, downloads the official resource, builds both data artifacts, runs validation, and writes the versioned outputs and metadata. The evaluation script implements a one-week horizon for 2025 and recomputes lag-1, lag-2, lag-4, four- and 12-week rolling means, and the previous-year same-week value at every forecast origin without reading the precomputed predictor columns. This approach makes the publication package independently rebuildable without distributing the upstream raw file \citep{Kim2022GitHub,Braga2023}.

Version-specific provenance is strengthened through cryptographic hashes. The source manifest records retrieval time, file size, and SHA-256 of the official Parquet snapshot; the final checksum file records hashes of the curated Parquet/CSV artifacts and principal metadata files. Similar checksum-based provenance has recently been used in a national health-data Data Descriptor to distinguish an immutable research release from mutable upstream public files \citep{Voinea2026}.

\label{sec:user-notes}

\subsection{Reproducing the Release}

Users can reproduce the complete release from a clean checkout with the commands below. The reproducible workflow uses Python 3.12.13 and the hash-verified \texttt{requirements.lock}; \texttt{requirements.txt} remains the editable declaration of minimum development requirements rather than the environment specification used for the release.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
git clone https://github.com/cvidalmsu/chile-ed-resp-benchmark.git
cd chile-ed-resp-benchmark
python -m venv .venv
# activate the environment for the operating system in use
python -m pip install --require-hashes -r requirements.lock
pytest -q
python scripts/make_release.py --start-year 2022 --end-year 2025 --force-download
\end{lstlisting}

Alternatively, repository maintainers can run \textit{Actions $\rightarrow$ Build DEIS-SADU dataset $\rightarrow$ Run workflow}. The action records the newly retrieved source checksum before rebuilding the release. For reuse and citation, users should reference the permanent \datasetname{} Version 1.1.0 archive at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22209795}, report the dataset version used in their analysis, and consult the public development repository at \url{\dataseturl} for subsequent updates.

\subsection{Recommended Forecasting Use}

For comparable model experiments, the recommended starting point is the forecasting artifact:
\begin{center}
\path{data/chile_ed_resp_ira_alta_forecasting.parquet}
\end{center}
Users seeking a high-completeness benchmark should initially restrict the table to the 178 establishments carrying the benchmark recommendation flag. The supplied \texttt{split} field provides a 2022--2024/2025 partition, but the valid interpretation of target-derived predictors depends on forecast design. For rolling-origin one-week-ahead evaluation, run \texttt{scripts/rolling\_origin\_example.py}; the script reconstructs the information set at each origin and exposes each recomputed predictor in \texttt{results/rolling\_origin\_predictions.csv}. For a fixed origin preceding 2025, later 2025 target-derived columns must not be used as known inputs; generate them recursively or select origin-available covariates. Randomly shuffled weekly observations are unsuitable for either design \citep{Hyndman2021}. High-dimensional models should be interpreted cautiously because model complexity and sample frequency can materially affect performance in emergency-demand forecasting \citep{Tuominen2022,Blanco2026}.

For descriptive analyses, the long table is preferable because it retains official age strata, cause wording, hospitalizations, and establishment metadata. Users should avoid summing consultation and hospitalization rows into a single outcome unless the analytical question explicitly requires such an aggregate. The \texttt{measure\_type}, \texttt{causa}, and \texttt{orden\_causa} fields make that distinction inspectable. Likewise, absent records must not be recoded as zeros without a justified external reporting model.

\subsection{Limitations and Reuse Hazards}

Several limitations should guide interpretation. First, the resource contains aggregate administrative counts rather than individual patient records; it cannot support patient-level risk prediction, causal inference, or analyses requiring comorbidity trajectories. Second, the meaning and completeness of administrative reporting depend on the upstream SADU system and local establishment practices. The pipeline reports anomalies but cannot independently verify whether every source count reflects complete clinical activity. Third, retrospective corrections by the public agency can change the current upstream file; source manifests and versioned releases are therefore essential when reproducing a published analysis.

Further limitations arise from temporal and modeling choices. The 2022--2025 window was selected to create a closed contemporary period, not to claim that earlier years are unusable. The 95\% IRA Alta coverage threshold is a reproducible benchmarking rule rather than a universal hospital-inclusion standard. Derived lags and rolling means remain missing when history is insufficient or when an exact required grid position is unavailable; gaps are never skipped or collapsed. Moreover, precomputed target-derived fields encode observations through the preceding row and therefore support sequential one-week origins, not a fixed-origin 53-week claim. No weather, staffing, bed-occupancy, holiday, or socioeconomic variable is added to the core release because such variables require separate provenance and licensing documentation.

Finally, the data should not be interpreted as a direct measure of latent population disease incidence. Emergency consultations reflect both morbidity and healthcare-seeking behavior, local service organization, referral patterns, and reporting practice. Forecasting models built from the resource are therefore best understood as models of reported emergency demand within the represented public-service context. \section{Conclusions}

In summary, \datasetname{} converts a valuable but operationally oriented Chilean public-health resource into a documented and reproducible research dataset without replacing official count observations. Version 1.1.0 comprises a 449,844-row, 32-variable long artifact and a 37,620-row, 38-variable hospital-week artifact covering 180 hospitals, 12 official labels, and 209 expected statistical weeks during 2022--2025. A fixed-denominator coverage rule identifies 178 hospitals for benchmarking while retaining all 180 establishments. The release provides a verbatim cause-to-variable mapping, reports coverage for all seven forecasting causes, distinguishes missing records from explicit zeros, preserves time-specific metadata, excludes hospitalization codes from consultation features, and provides a one-week rolling-origin example. Fifteen validation checks pass with no warnings or failures, and their record scopes and zero-valued exception counts are reported explicitly. Source manifests, checksums, descriptive audits, tests, licensing guidance, and automated rebuilding support traceability from DEIS/SADU to the versioned release.
